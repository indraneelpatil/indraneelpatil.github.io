My mom gifted me a few of her plants for my new place. Unfortunately after a few months three of the plants died which made my mom extremely mad. I have taken good care of plants before in the US where none of my plants ever died. So inspite of my mom telling me otherwise I decided to stick to the same care routine that I used to follow in the US. Unfortuntely India is much hotter than the US so plants here need much more water than they do there. So in this case I should have really just listened to my mom instead of sticking to my guts based on past experience which clearly would not transfer.

I have done similar things in the past where if someone wants me to do something which I dont actually agree on, I wont do it until I am myself convinced that it is the right thing to do. This lands me in trouble sometimes as it did in the plant care story but sometimes I turn out right. Furthermore it really drives my friends and family mad and they call me "inflexible". In summary I have strong priors on things and my priors dont change until my own experience or a rational fallacy tells me to otherwise. And I usually act according to my priors. On things where I have no priors, I am probably pretty flexible and pretty quick to fall in line. 

The good part about this is this makes me very less gullible and makes it harder for me to believe in the wrong things. The bad part is I could be stuck doing something suboptimal for much longer and far more likely to learn things the hard way by getting burnt. As in this case I didnt have to lose three plants to start taking better care of my plants. Sometimes learning by experience is far too costly. Imagine someone starts driving their car really fast because they start believing that they are a really good driver because they have never had an accident inspite of everyone telling you how bad driving fast is. They are bound to get into a really bad accident. If the accident isnt super bad then they may get a second chance at correcting their priors but life isnt always that forgiving.

I think its good to have strong priors but it is also super important to critically consider the conflicting advice someone else is giving you and really think about the context and where they are coming from and what their experience in the matter is. Coming back to the plant example, I weighed my priors only while making my decision but I never considered how much experience I really have in taking care of plants in India and how much experience my mom has in this. In theory I should have weighed my moms priors much higher than mine with all things considered that my mom obviously has no selfish motives here. I am gonna call this being "Flexibly Stubborn".
